# ROC charter

**Remote Operations Center (ROC)** — Marine Technologies

## Purpose

ROC is Marine Technologies’ shore-side **Remote Operations Center**. It is both:

- a **department** that unifies customer support, Information Technology (IT) for remote services, vessel remote monitoring, vessel remote operations support, and governance of data from vessel to shore; and
- the **designated Remote-Control Station** in the official Concept of Operations (ConOps) submitted for American Bureau of Shipping (ABS) and DNV review — a constantly manned shore watch that monitors and assists **crewed** vessels.

The current class concept is **supervised remote-assisted operation**. Minimum crew and the master’s command stay aboard. The ROC does not replace the vessel as the default controller.

See [class concept — assisted remote operations](class-and-conops.md).

## In scope

- Unified coordination of **customer-facing** and **internal** remote operations touchpoints (routing, escalation, handoffs).
- **Monitoring and situational awareness** for remotely supervised systems, consistent with product, contractual, and class obligations.
- **Assisted remote operations** within approved runbooks: observe, advise, and — only when separately authorized — limited remote action, with onboard override always available.
- **Data path stewardship**: ingestion, classification, retention posture, and access aligned with company policy and agreements—not ad hoc spreadsheets or personal drives.
- **Runbooks and procedures** for repeatable responses (incidents, office-hours continuity, control transfer, escalations).

## Out of scope (unless explicitly assigned)

- Replacing **product engineering** sign-off for safety-critical control changes.
- **On-vessel** physical work, crewing decisions, or reducing manning because the ROC is watching.
- Taking or keeping remote control when the crew overrides, the link is lost, or transfer is not confirmed.
- Treating **fully autonomous** (ABS AUTONOMOUS) functions as day-to-day work before class and the General Manager release that step.
- **Legal or contractual** interpretation without involvement of authorized stakeholders—ROC documents facts and timelines; Legal/Commercial own commitments.

## Principles

1. **Safety and compliance first** — No remote action without clear authorization per product, class, and regulatory rules. One controller at a time; loss of link reverts to the vessel.
2. **Single coherent customer experience** — One front door for operational inquiries where practical; no conflicting answers across teams.
3. **Data discipline** — Minimum necessary access; audit-friendly handling; no secrets in general documentation repos.
4. **Measurable operations** — Goals use Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) and Service Level Agreements (SLAs) that can be reviewed quarterly.
5. **Time for prevention** — Improve work / time efficiency so saved capacity goes to planning and preventing problems, not only to fixing them. Year-one **commit** is a **20%** time-per-task gain and about **15%** of time on prevention; **30%** remains the stretch. See [year one goals](../goals/year-one-goals.md).

## Reporting line

ROC **Supervisor** reports to the **General Manager** (see [org-chart.md](../org/org-chart.md)).

## Review cycle

Update this charter when scope, regulatory context, or General Manager expectations change—at least **annually**, or after major reorganizations.
